AI should be strictly regulated because its risks are not theoretical—they are immediate, масштабed, and hard to reverse.

First, AI can cause real harm at extraordinary speed. Unlike most technologies, AI can generate misinformation, fraud, deepfakes, cyberattacks, and harmful decision-making at scale, instantly and cheaply. A single powerful system can influence millions of people, undermine elections, or help criminals automate abuse. When the potential damage is this large, “move fast and break things” is reckless.

Second, AI systems are too opaque to trust without oversight. Even their creators often cannot fully explain why a model made a particular decision. That is dangerous in areas like hiring, policing, healthcare, finance, and education, where biased or incorrect outputs can ruin lives. Strict regulation is necessary to require transparency, testing, audits, and accountability before deployment.

Third, without rules, companies have strong incentives to prioritize profit over safety. History shows that industries rarely self-regulate effectively when speed and competition are rewarded. If regulation is weak, firms will rush products to market before they are safe, because the cost of failure falls on the public, not the developers.

Finally, strict regulation does not mean banning innovation. It means setting clear guardrails: safety testing, liability standards, transparency requirements, human oversight, and limits on high-risk uses. This protects society while still allowing beneficial AI to develop responsibly.

In short, AI is powerful enough to improve lives—but also powerful enough to harm them at scale. That is exactly why it must be strictly regulated.
