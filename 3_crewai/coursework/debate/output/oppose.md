I oppose the motion.

AI should **not** be strictly regulated because overregulation would not make us safer—it would make us **slower, weaker, and less competitive** while failing to stop the real bad actors anyway.

First, strict regulation will mostly burden the people trying to use AI responsibly, not the criminals who misuse it. Fraudsters, cybercriminals, and hostile states will not wait for permits, compliance forms, or approval boards. They will use the technology regardless. The people who will be restrained are startups, researchers, hospitals, schools, and smaller firms that could use AI to improve productivity, medicine, and education. That means strict regulation punishes good actors while doing little to stop malicious ones.

Second, AI moves too fast for heavy-handed regulation to keep up. By the time laws are written, negotiated, and enforced, the technology will have changed. Strict rules risk freezing innovation around outdated assumptions. In practice, this creates a system where only the largest companies can afford compliance, which entrenches monopolies instead of protecting the public.

Third, the answer to AI risk is **targeted oversight**, not strict regulation of the whole field. We already regulate harmful outcomes in many sectors: fraud, discrimination, privacy violations, unsafe products, and cybercrime. That is better than blanket restrictions on an entire general-purpose technology. We should punish misuse and high-risk deployment, not choke off the broader benefits of AI.

Finally, AI is too important to smother with fear. It can accelerate medical discovery, improve logistics, help students, support accessibility, and raise economic growth. Strict regulation would slow these benefits for everyone based on worst-case scenarios. We should manage risk intelligently, not respond with blanket constraint.

So the real choice is not safety versus chaos. It is **smart, flexible governance versus rigid regulation that protects incumbents, slows progress, and still leaves the worst actors untouched**. For that reason, AI should not be strictly regulated.
